The ESP32's ADC is good enough for most sensor work. Most bad readings we see come from four mistakes, and all four are cheap to avoid.

## 1. Use ADC1 when Wi-Fi is on

The ESP32 has two ADCs, and ADC2 is shared with the Wi-Fi driver. On the original ESP32, ADC2 reads fail while Wi-Fi is running. On the ESP32-C3, one-shot reads on ADC2 are not supported at all.

Put analog sensors on ADC1 pins: GPIO32 to GPIO39 on the ESP32, GPIO0 to GPIO4 on the ESP32-C3.

## 2. Know the range of each attenuation setting

Attenuation trades range for resolution. The ESP-IDF documentation gives the range in which readings are accurate:

| Attenuation | ESP32 | ESP32-S3 | ESP32-C3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 dB | 100 to 950 mV | 0 to 950 mV | 0 to 750 mV |
| 2.5 dB | 100 to 1250 mV | 0 to 1250 mV | 0 to 1050 mV |
| 6 dB | 150 to 1750 mV | 0 to 1750 mV | 0 to 1300 mV |
| 12 dB | 150 to 2450 mV | 0 to 3100 mV | 0 to 2500 mV |

Two traps sit in this table. On the original ESP32, readings below 100 to 150 mV are not reliable, so a sensor that rests near 0 V reads worst exactly where you care most. And a 5 V sensor output needs a divider to fit; the [divider tool](/tools/voltage-divider) does the arithmetic and checks the range for you.

## 3. Calibrate in software

Raw counts are not millivolts. Every chip's ADC has its own offset and gain, and ESP-IDF's calibration schemes use values burned in at the factory to convert one to the other. Create a calibration handle (line fitting or curve fitting, depending on the chip) and convert with `adc_cali_raw_to_voltage()` instead of scaling raw counts yourself.

## 4. Give the ADC a quiet, low-impedance signal

The ADC samples by charging a small internal capacitor. A divider made of hundreds of kilohms can't charge it fully between samples, and readings sag.

- Put a 100 nF capacitor from the ADC pin to ground, close to the pin.
- Average 8 to 16 samples per reading. It costs microseconds and removes most of the noise.
- If readings jump while Wi-Fi transmits, take them before the radio starts.

## What we do now

ADC1 pins only. A divider sized to the recommended range. A capacitor at the pin. The calibration API, an averaged reading, and on battery nodes, a reading taken before the radio wakes.
